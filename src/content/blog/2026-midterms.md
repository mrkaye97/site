It's election day tomorrow, and I figured I'd write something about it, because I've been thinking about it incessantly for the past few weeks (months?), as is tradition. I've written a lot here about tech, engineering, etc., but my first true love is very much still analytics, statistics, and forecasting, and I always find myself frustrated around this time of year by how much misleading (read: bad) information there is out there. Or to be less diplomatic, just how many bad takes there are.

Right now, it looks like a blue wave. If you've been following [FLIPR](https://www.natesilver.net/p/nate-silver-2026-midterm-election-polls-model), which is Nate Silver's 2026 forecast model, then you know it's looked this way for a while. In recent years, people have been, understandably, scared of using that term. Probably because of some scar tissue from 2016, when FiveThirtyEight gave Trump about a 30% chance of winning the election. Note that other outlets had his odds much lower, such as the NYT (16% on Monday) and the Huffington Post (2%, which is, frankly, irresponsible in any environment). Since then, I've heard a lot of commentary about how "the polls are wrong every year" and "Nate Silver was wrong in 2016" basically equating "there is polling error" to "there is no useful information in polling."

I've written about [cognitive biases](/blog/unconference-2026) a little bit before. You might've heard of this sort of bias referred to as the "[nirvana fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_fallacy)," basically the rejection of something that mostly works but is imperfect (polling) because of an ideal.

This frustrates me to no end, because in recent years, polling has been 1) largely pretty reliable and 2) has not been particularly biased in a consistent way (low on Republicans in 2016 and 2020, low on Democrats in 2018 and 2022), although people on both sides of the aisle seem to want to believe that it's always overestimated their own party. So, often, people want to disregard the polling in favor of blind pessimism, probably so that they reserve the right to say "I knew we shouldn't have listened to those damned pollsters!" when the election breaks the other way, and "phew, that was close - glad it worked out" when it goes their way. Win-win!

Tomorrow, I'm expecting a blue wave (FLIPR has blue senate odds around 80%, and the betting markets are around 75), and I suspect we'll have a pretty good sense of how things are going relatively early on, especially by looking at Florida's results as they come in. Since Florida's polls close at 7pm (8pm in the panhandle), they've historically been pretty fast at counting ballots, and they have a not-particularly-close senate race (Ashley Moody strongly favored over Angie Nixon), we might have a sense for how things are going to turn out over the course of the night if, e.g., Nixon performs particularly well or poorly relative to her polls. I'll be stressing, and reading the [GD Politics live blog](https://www.gdpolitics.com/), and probably having a drink or two.

Cheers to another election season!
